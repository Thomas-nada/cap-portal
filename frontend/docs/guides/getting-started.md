# Getting Started and Using the Portal

This guide covers the complete path from arriving at the CAP Portal to participating in a proposal and following it through the workflow.

---

## 1. Connect a wallet

You can read the portal without signing in. To submit a proposal, comment, or take part in a discussion, connect a CIP-30 compatible Cardano browser wallet such as Eternl, Lace, Vespr, Typhon, Flint, or Nami.

1. Select **Connect Wallet** in the navigation.
2. Choose a detected wallet.
3. Approve the request to sign a message.
4. Choose a display name if prompted.

Signing proves that you control the wallet address. It does not create a transaction, charge a fee, or give the portal access to your private keys, seed phrase, or funds.

## 2. Browse the portal

Use the main navigation to explore:

- **Home** for an overview and recent activity.
- **Proposals** for the searchable list of CAPs and CISs.
- **Board** to see proposals grouped by workflow stage.
- **Constitution** to read the current text and compare proposal drafts.
- **Guides** for the reviewed launch documentation.

Open any proposal to read its summary, proposed changes, supporting analysis, current labels, discussion, and audit history.

## 3. Submit a CAP or CIS

Select **New Proposal** and follow the guided form.

1. Choose **CAP** or **CIS**, select a category, and enter a clear title.
2. For a CAP, highlight the exact constitutional text you want to replace or add after.
3. Enter the proposed wording for each selected passage.
4. Explain the proposal with a summary, motivation or problem statement, analysis, impact, and any supporting links.
5. Review the preview and submit.

A **CAP** proposes a specific constitutional change. A **CIS** documents a constitutional issue without requiring a proposed solution. See [CAPs and CISs Explained](/#/guides/intro-to-caps-and-cis) for help choosing between them.

New proposals are published in **consultation** and become available for community discussion. A CAP with constitutional revisions also receives a draft Constitution version so readers can inspect the proposed text in context.

## 4. Comment and discuss

Anyone can read proposal discussions. A connected wallet is required to post.

1. Open a proposal.
2. Scroll to **Discussion**.
3. Write and submit your comment.

Good comments identify the exact wording or issue being discussed, explain the reasoning, and offer a constructive alternative where possible. Keep the discussion focused on the proposal and support factual claims with useful references.

Editors may also leave structured suggestions on individual proposal fields. The author can accept or reject each suggestion. Comments, suggestions, and workflow changes remain visible in the proposal's audit history.

## 5. Follow the workflow

Every proposal has one lifecycle stage:

| Stage | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Consultation** | Open for community deliberation and editor review. |
| **Ready** | Deliberation is complete and the proposal is ready for the next governance step. |
| **Done** | The portal process has concluded. |
| **Withdrawn** | The author has closed the proposal. |

Authors can signal that their proposal is ready to advance. Editors review its completeness and process requirements before moving it to the next stage. Editors facilitate the process; they do not take ownership of the author's proposal or decide whether its substance should be adopted.

The Board provides the quickest overview of where proposals sit. Proposal pages show additional status tags, editor signals, and category information when relevant.

## A few useful points

- Reading does not require a wallet.
- Submitting and commenting do require a wallet connection.
- Authors can revise a proposal during consultation and should explain substantial changes in the discussion.
- Authors retain ownership and may withdraw their proposals.
- Completing the portal workflow does not itself amend the Constitution; any required on-chain governance process remains separate.
