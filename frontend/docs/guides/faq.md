# Frequently Asked Questions

## What is a CAP?

A Constitutional Amendment Proposal is a structured proposal for a specific change, clarification, or addition to the Cardano Constitution or its governance process. It identifies the affected text and proposes the new wording.

## What is a CIS?

A Constitutional Issue Statement documents an ambiguity, gap, or governance risk without requiring a proposed solution. A CIS can help the community agree on a problem before one or more CAPs propose ways to solve it.

## Which one should I use?

Use a CAP when you have a specific constitutional change to propose. Use a CIS when the problem needs to be documented or discussed before a solution is ready. See [CAPs and CISs Explained](/#/guides/intro-to-caps-and-cis) for a fuller comparison.

## Who can submit a CAP or CIS?

Any community member who can connect a supported Cardano wallet may submit. No editor approval, specialist credential, or separate portal account is required.

## Do I need a wallet?

You do not need a wallet to browse proposals, the Constitution, guides, or public discussions. You do need one to submit proposals, comment, and perform actions tied to an identity. Connecting signs a message; it does not create a transaction or expose your private keys.

## How does the proposal form work?

The form guides you through choosing the proposal type and category, selecting constitutional text for a CAP, drafting the proposed change, explaining the proposal, reviewing it, and submitting it. The [Getting Started and Using the Portal](/#/guides/getting-started) guide covers the complete flow.

## Can I edit my proposal?

Authors can revise their proposals during consultation. Significant changes should be explained in the discussion so participants and editors know what changed. Proposals that have reached a closed lifecycle stage cannot be edited.

## Can a CIS become a CAP?

Not directly. They remain separate records: the CIS documents the problem and a CAP proposes a solution. One CIS may lead to several competing CAPs, and a CAP may address more than one CIS.

## How long does consultation last?

The recommended period depends on the proposal category and complexity. The period is a minimum opportunity for review, not an automatic deadline. A proposal does not advance merely because time has elapsed.

## What happens after consultation?

The author signals readiness and an editor reviews whether the proposal is complete and ready for the next stage. If further work is needed, it stays in consultation. When the process requirements are satisfied, an editor can move it to **Ready**.

## Do editors approve the substance of proposals?

No. Editors facilitate a consistent, transparent, and reviewable process. They can identify concerns and suggest changes, but authors retain ownership and decide whether to accept suggestions. The [Editor Role and Responsibilities](/#/guides/editor-role) guide explains the boundary in detail.

## Does reaching Done change the Constitution?

No. The portal supports off-chain drafting, deliberation, and preparation. Any constitutional change that requires an on-chain governance action must still go through the applicable Cardano governance process.

## Can an author withdraw a proposal?

Yes. Authors may withdraw their own proposal. The withdrawn proposal remains part of the public history rather than being silently deleted.

## Where can I see a proposal's status?

The current lifecycle and labels appear on proposal cards and proposal pages. The Board groups proposals by lifecycle stage and is the quickest way to see the overall workflow.
